# M8 implementation specification: chunked prefill and robust scheduling

- Status: proposed implementation contract
- Roadmap milestone: M8 in [`docs/plan.md`](../plan.md)
- Prerequisite: completed [M7](m7.md), including M1-M6 transitively
- Immediate consumer: M9 sampling and HTTP serving
- Later consumers: M10 operations, M11 prefix caching, M12 CUDA graphs, and M14 worker processes

> **Re-planning note (2026-09-03, ADR 0042):** the M1 CPU
> scheduler simulator this specification assumed as its offline oracle was removed. Simulator-based
> gates, tools, stages, and imported-workload plans below are void until re-planned against real
> execution (serving benchmarks, recorded traces, and soak suites).


This document turns M8 into an executable engineering plan. It specifies page-aligned chunked
prefill, decode progress protection, deterministic priority aging, deadline-aware admission,
low-water recompute preemption, exact work counters, transactional scheduling, policy snapshots,
tests, evidence, and the handoff to M9.

M8 changes scheduling policy and request progress. It does not add serving protocols, new sampling
algorithms, prefix sharing, speculative decoding, or another owner of mutable engine state.

## 1. Outcome

At the end of M8, InferX can:

1. split long prompt or recomputation work into finite page-aware chunks without changing logits or
   emitted tokens;
2. reserve useful progress for ready decode requests before filling the remaining step budget with
   prefill work;
3. run a mixed prefill/decode step when the selected backend explicitly supports it, or use a
   starvation-free alternating policy when it does not;
4. rank eligible requests by bounded priority aging, deadline state, and stable arrival identity;
5. reject a deadline that is already impossible under a documented conservative estimate, while
   treating accepted deadlines as best-effort rather than a completion guarantee;
6. react to M6 low-water pressure by deterministically selecting ready victims, retaining only a
   valid page-aligned prefix, releasing the remainder, and rebuilding missing KV from known tokens;
7. distinguish cached, computed, scheduled, and committed progress exactly at every transition;
8. prepare all request, KV, workspace, plan-buffer, response, and preemption mutations before one
   no-fail commit, with complete rollback on rejection;
9. apply one immutable, versioned scheduling-policy snapshot per coordinator turn and reproduce every
   decision from a recorded trace; and
10. compare baseline and candidate policies on imported workloads using correctness, fairness, SLO,
    latency, utilization, and scheduler-cost evidence.

```text
ready request snapshot + immutable policy snapshot + resource snapshot
                              |
                              v
                rank and reserve decode progress
                              |
                              v
              fill remaining budget with prefill chunks
                              |
             low water or blocked allocation?
                    | yes                 | no
                    v                     v
        prepare deterministic victim      prepare append/workspace/plan
        and retained-prefix transaction   transactions
                    \                     /
                     v                   v
                  validate the complete proposal
                              |
                    backend accepts submission
                              |
                 no-fail commit of all ownership
                              |
                 completion validates exact identity
                              |
              commit KV/progress/output or abort exactly
```

M8 is complete only when chunked and preempted execution is token-identical to uninterrupted M7,
eligible work cannot starve under the declared policy, and every rollback/preemption path restores
all resource and progress counters exactly.

### Implementation status (2026-08-31)

This file is a documentation specification only. This change does not authorize or add M8 source,
test, build, or benchmark implementation. The implementation must extend the M1/M6/M7 contracts;
it must not introduce a second request state machine, scheduler-owned KV allocator, or unbounded
queue.

## 2. Scope boundaries

### 2.1 In scope

- One coordinator and one device worker, with M7's single in-flight GPU step retained.
- Chunked processing of a prompt and of a known token history being recomputed after preemption.
- Page-aligned non-final chunk boundaries; a final logical tail may be shorter than one page.
- A per-step token budget shared by prefill, recompute, and one-token decode items.
- Decode progress reservation and a bounded forced-prefill rule.
- Capability-selected mixed batches and deterministic alternating-phase fallback.
- Integer request priority, bounded aging, deadline classification, and stable tie-breaking.
- M6 low-water input, deterministic recompute-victim selection, page-safe retained prefix, and
  resource reclamation.
- Exact counter/state rules and an atomic two-phase proposal/reservation/commit protocol.
- Immutable policy configuration snapshots, trace schema evolution, CPU replay, workload import,
  policy comparison, stress, correctness, and performance evidence.

### 2.2 Explicitly out of scope

- OpenAI-compatible HTTP, SSE, authentication, rate limiting, tenants, or network admission; M9 owns
  the protocol surface.
- Cross-request prefix caching or page sharing. A retained prefix belongs only to the same request;
  M11 owns reusable prefix caches.
- Beam search, sampling beyond M5 greedy behavior, speculative decoding, or rollback of already
  emitted tokens.
- CUDA graphs, multi-step execution, more than one in-flight GPU batch, multi-GPU routing, or
  distributed scheduling.
- Priority donation between requests, per-tenant weighted fair queuing, preempting in-flight GPU
  work, or hard real-time deadline guarantees.
- Evicting pages whose last write is not fenced, retaining a partial tail, accepting a completion
  with an old epoch, or reconstructing tokens that are not present in immutable prompt/committed
  output storage.
- Runtime policy mutation from the device worker or observability threads.

## 6. Policy configuration and validation

M8 adds a versioned `scheduler.policy` section. Defaults are candidates until the M8 benchmark ADR
records results on the reference GPU; checked-in defaults must match that decision.

| Field | Initial candidate / range | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `mode` | `alternating`; `alternating` or `mixed` | `mixed` accepted only when the prepared backend key declares support |
| `prefill_chunk_tokens` | 256; one page through step token limit | resolved to a positive page multiple |
| `max_scheduled_tokens_per_step` | inherited M7 value | aggregate prefill, recompute, and decode positions |
| `decode_reserve_sequences` | 32; 0 through decode step limit | maximum ready decodes protected before prefill fill |
| `max_consecutive_decode_steps` | 8; 1 through 1024 | forces eligible prefill/recompute progress in alternating mode |
| `max_consecutive_prefill_steps` | 1; 1 through 1024 | forces ready decode progress when reserve is nonzero |
| `priority_min` / `priority_max` | -8 / 8 | higher value ranks first; submitted value must be in range |
| `aging_quantum_ms` | 100; 1 through 60,000 | each complete ready-wait quantum adds one effective level |
| `aging_cap` | 16; 0 through 1,000,000 | bounds boost without rewriting base priority |
| `deadline_guard_ms` | 5; 0 through 60,000 | added to the feasibility estimate |
| `deadline_estimator` | `conservative_ewma_v1` | named, versioned formula and initialized fallback rates |
| `preemption_enabled` | true | may act only on ready, unpinned victims |
| `preemption_keep_blocks` | 0; 0 through table maximum | upper bound; reclaim requirement can force fewer retained blocks |
| `preemption_min_reclaim_blocks` | 1 | do not churn a victim that cannot meet useful reclaim |
| `preemption_max_victims_per_turn` | 8; 1 through active limit | bounds coordinator work and trace size |
| `low_water_exit_hysteresis_blocks` | one step worst-case append | prevents repeated enter/exit oscillation |
| `policy_update_enabled` | false by default | update is an explicit validated coordinator command |

Validation occurs after model context, M6 page size/capacity, M4 batch capability, workspace limits,
and M7 queue capacities are known. Startup rejects:

- a chunk size that is not a page multiple or exceeds the step token budget;
- a decode reserve above sequence or scheduled-token capacity;
- a forced-phase interval of zero;
- a priority domain, duration, or multiplication that can overflow checked arithmetic;
- mixed mode without one selected backend for every admitted mixed shape;
- a retained-block limit above table/context capacity;
- a low-water threshold plus hysteresis that leaves no usable allocation range; or
- an estimator without initialized rates for every supported work class.

Resolved values, source precedence, schema version, model/page/backend fingerprints, and the policy
fingerprint are logged once and stored in every trace header. Environment aliases and silent clamping
are not accepted.

## 7. Immutable policy snapshots

A `PolicySnapshot` is an immutable, reference-counted value prepared off the scheduling hot path and
installed by the coordinator only between turns when no proposal is partially prepared. It contains:

- schema version and monotonically increasing policy generation;
- all resolved fields in section 6;
- page size, model context, prepared batch capabilities, and resource capacity fingerprints;
- initialized and current estimator coefficients/rates with their sample ordinal;
- the timestamp/clock domain at which it becomes active; and
- a canonical serialization and digest.

One coordinator turn reads exactly one snapshot. Admission, ranking, chunk selection, preemption,
plan construction, metrics attribution, and trace records for that turn use the same generation.
An accepted in-flight plan keeps its snapshot reference through completion and acknowledgement.

A runtime update is handled as follows:

1. parse into a new value without mutating the active policy;
2. validate against immutable engine/model capabilities and current bounded capacities;
3. assign a new generation and canonical digest;
4. enqueue an ordered coordinator command;
5. install at the next turn boundary and emit `PolicyInstalled`; and
6. retain the old snapshot while any ticket or replay record references it.

Invalid updates leave the active generation unchanged. Update, cancellation, deadline, and
completion order follows M7's coordinator event precedence. Configuration changes never reinterpret
already committed counters or change an accepted plan.

## 8. Request progress model and exact counters

M8 uses distinct counters; no field may be inferred from state alone.

| Counter | Meaning | Mutation point |
|---|---|---|
| `prompt_tokens` | immutable prompt length | admission only |
| `committed_output_tokens` | output tokens accepted into the request result | successful decode/final-prefill completion only |
| `target_history_tokens` | known tokens whose KV must exist before the next decode | prompt plus the committed outputs required as decode input |
| `cached_tokens` | page-aligned same-request prefix reused when an epoch starts/restarts | admission or reclaim commit; fixed within that epoch |
| `computed_tokens` | contiguous logical history whose KV is committed and visible | successful append completion or reclaim commit |
| `scheduled_tokens` | outstanding logical positions owned by exactly one in-flight ticket | accepted submission until completion/abort |
| `recompute_target_tokens` | pre-preemption computed high-water to rebuild before normal work | preemption commit until catch-up |

`cached_tokens` is same-request retained work before M11, not cross-request prefix-cache credit. It
records how much of the epoch's starting logical prefix did not need recomputation. `computed_tokens`
records the complete currently visible KV prefix, including cached and newly computed work.

### 8.1 Counter invariants

For every live request:

- all counters use checked token-count types and are nonnegative;
- `cached_tokens <= computed_tokens <= target_history_tokens <= model_context_tokens`;
- `cached_tokens` is zero or a multiple of page size and stays constant within one request epoch;
- during an in-flight append, public committed/computed values remain unchanged and
  `scheduled_tokens` equals the ticket's logical range length;
- without an in-flight ticket, `scheduled_tokens == 0`;
- the M6 block table committed length equals `computed_tokens` exactly;
- immutable output-vector length equals `committed_output_tokens`;
- `recompute_target_tokens` is zero outside recomputation; while recomputing it is at least
  `computed_tokens` and no greater than the known history length captured at preemption;
- a request can schedule only the contiguous range beginning at `computed_tokens`; and
- cancellation/failure can clear scheduled work only through the matching completion/abort path.

The coordinator's invariant dump reports all seven values plus table length, quota, physical pages,
pending append, ticket identity, request state/epoch, and policy generation. Tests compare this dump
at every randomized step.

### 8.2 Logical history

Logical history is the immutable prompt followed by committed generated token IDs. Recomputation
reads token IDs directly from these owned vectors. It never re-tokenizes text and never changes an
already committed output token.

The next normal decode may begin only when KV covers every logical input token required by M5's
decode contract. If the just-generated token is appended on the next decode step, the target and
scheduled range follow that existing convention consistently; the ADR records the convention and
all formula fixtures use it. M8 may not mix two conventions in traces or counters.

## 9. Work kinds and lifecycle extensions

M8 recognizes three scheduling work kinds:

- **prompt prefill:** consume a prompt range that has never been computed for this request epoch;
- **recompute prefill:** rebuild a known prompt/output history range after preemption without
  sampling, emitting, or changing request output; and
- **decode:** consume the next decode input and commit at most one new output under M5 rules.

The M1/M7 lifecycle is extended, not replaced:

```text
PrefillReady -> Prefilling -> PrefillReady       intermediate successful prompt chunk
PrefillReady -> Prefilling -> DecodeReady        final prompt chunk, request continues
PrefillReady -> Prefilling -> Finishing          final prompt chunk also finishes by policy

ready -> Preempted -> Queued -> Reserving -> RecomputeReady
RecomputeReady -> Recomputing -> RecomputeReady  intermediate rebuild chunk
RecomputeReady -> Recomputing -> original ready kind when catch-up completes
```

If the existing enum must remain ABI-stable, recompute readiness may be represented by a typed
substate of prefill readiness, but traces and validators must still distinguish prompt from
recompute work. Raw enum/state writes remain forbidden.

An intermediate prompt or recompute completion:

- validates the full M7/M8 completion identity;
- commits the matching M6 append;
- advances `computed_tokens` by exactly the scheduled range;
- clears `scheduled_tokens`;
- emits no token/text response and does not evaluate EOS;
- returns to the corresponding ready state; and
- retains original arrival identity and ready-wait accounting as specified in section 11.

Only the final prompt chunk requests the final hidden/logits row needed to produce the first output.
Recompute chunks never produce logits for sampling, including their final chunk.

## 10. Page-aligned chunk calculation

Let:

- `P` be the logical target for the current prompt or recompute phase;
- `C` be committed `computed_tokens`;
- `L` be the resolved chunk-token limit;
- `B` be remaining aggregate scheduled-token budget;
- `Q` be the M6 page size; and
- `K` be the work-kind-specific remaining context/resource capacity.

The candidate length is the minimum of `P-C`, `L`, `B`, and `K`. Selection follows these exact rules:

1. If `P == C`, no chunk is emitted; lifecycle catch-up occurs before selection.
2. If `C + candidate == P`, select the final tail exactly, even when its length or ending
   position is not page-aligned.
3. Otherwise, round the candidate end down to the greatest boundary divisible by `Q`.
4. If rounding produces no progress, the request does not fit this step. The scheduler tries another
   eligible item or phase; it never emits a zero-length plan item.
5. The selected range is exactly `[C, end)`, appears once, and its length is charged to every token,
   workspace, metadata, and append budget before selection continues.
6. Checked arithmetic failure or an end beyond target/context is a plan-validation error, not a
   reason to clamp.

Consequences:

- every non-final chunk ends at a page boundary;
- a final prompt/recompute tail may leave one exclusive partial page, matching M6's fragmentation
  bound;
- a configured chunk smaller than a page is invalid rather than silently enlarged;
- an available per-step budget smaller than a page cannot start a non-final prefill; and
- decode reservation is subtracted before `B` is offered to prefill when policy requires it.

Boundary fixtures cover empty rejection, one token, `Q-1`, `Q`, `Q+1`, `L-1`, `L`, `L+1`, context
edge, budget edge, integer edge, and a final tail after every possible page offset.

## 11. Deterministic fairness and ready ordering

### 11.1 Base priority and aging

Applications may submit an integer base priority within the configured inclusive domain. Higher is
more important. Missing priority is zero. Admission rejects an out-of-range value; it does not clamp.

For a request ready at coordinator time `r` and evaluated at time `now`, the waiting age is the
nonnegative, saturating difference `now-r`. The aging boost is the number of complete aging quanta,
capped by `aging_cap`. Effective priority is the checked sum of base priority and boost, saturated
only at the explicitly documented effective upper bound.

Ready time means continuous eligibility for useful work. It resets after a request makes successful
progress, after it becomes temporarily ineligible, or after it is requeued from preemption. It does
not reset merely because a planning proposal was rejected or another item won the step. Clock
regression is a runtime invariant failure in production and an explicit replay fault in tests.

### 11.2 Stable total order

Within a work class, eligible requests sort by:

1. effective priority descending;
2. deadline class: urgent feasible, ordinary feasible, then no deadline;
3. absolute deadline ascending within deadline-bearing classes;
4. ready time ascending;
5. M1 `ArrivalKey` ascending; and
6. strong request ID as the final total-order tie-breaker.

The deadline class is computed once from the turn's clock and estimator snapshot. An unordered map's
iteration order, thread completion order, pointer value, or floating-point comparison may never be a
tie-breaker.

### 11.3 Starvation bound

Bounded aging ensures a continuously eligible low-priority request eventually ties the maximum
effective priority. Stable ready/arrival order then moves it ahead of later peers. Phase rules add:

- when decode is ready and decode reserve is nonzero, no more than
  `max_consecutive_prefill_steps` accepted prefill-only steps may occur before a decode-capable step;
- when prefill/recompute is ready, no more than `max_consecutive_decode_steps` accepted decode-only
  steps may occur before a step allocates at least one page-sized chunk, if such a chunk fits; and
- a forced phase that cannot form a valid item does not consume/reset the opposing counter.

The proof assumes finite configured maximum request length, a functioning executor, resource
availability eventually recurring, and no cancellation/deadline expiry. These are the definition of
an **eligible** request in the 30-minute fairness gate.

## 12. Deadline-aware admission and scheduling

Deadlines use M7's monotonic clock and are absolute within that clock domain. They are optional and
remain best-effort after acceptance.

### 12.1 Feasibility estimate

The versioned estimator uses only values captured in the turn's snapshot:

- queued/running prompt and recompute token work ahead under the selected priority policy;
- protected decode work ahead;
- this request's remaining prompt and configured maximum output work;
- conservative per-class service rates derived from bounded EWMA observations or startup fallback;
- fixed coordinator/submission overhead; and
- `deadline_guard_ms`.

Rate updates occur only from successful, non-warmup measurements, are clamped to validated bounds,
and are installed in a future snapshot. The plan being measured never changes its own estimate.
Replay records the already-resolved estimator inputs, so host timing noise cannot change a decision.

Admission rejects with a stable `deadline_impossible` error when either the deadline is at/past the
observed admission time or the conservative earliest-finish estimate exceeds it. Overflow is treated
as impossible. A rejected request consumes no active slot, quota, mailbox, or tokenization job beyond
the bounded submission receipt already owned by M7.

Token-ID requests run this check during their first coordinator admission turn. Text/chat requests
whose exact prompt length is not yet known receive only M7's bounded provisional submission receipt;
they run the final feasibility check after successful tokenization and before queue/KV admission.
Expiry while tokenizing follows M7 cancellation/deadline cleanup. This two-stage rule prevents the
estimator from guessing a token count and ensures an impossible text request never owns KV quota.

### 12.2 Ready-time deadline behavior

At every coordinator turn, expired requests are processed using M7 event precedence before new plan
commit. A request estimated to miss after admission is marked at-risk for ranking/metrics; it is not
silently failed before its actual deadline. Urgency is the remaining slack relative to one estimated
service quantum. Deadline ranking cannot bypass a higher effective priority class unless the accepted
ADR explicitly enables that behavior; the initial policy keeps priority first.

Metrics distinguish impossible-at-admission, expired in queue, expired ready, expired in flight and
drained, completed on time, completed late due to same-turn ordering, and estimate false positive/
negative. No metric claims hard real-time conformance.

## 13. Decode progress reservation and phase policy

### 13.1 Reservation

At the start of plan construction, collect eligible decode requests in section 11 order. Protect up
to the minimum of:

- ready decode count;
- `decode_reserve_sequences`;
- maximum sequence slots;
- scheduled-token budget; and
- workspace/metadata/backend limits.

Each protected decode consumes one scheduled position under M5's convention. Protection is a
planning entitlement, not visible resource ownership: if the complete proposal cannot prepare, the
request remains unchanged. Unused protected capacity is returned to the common budget after all
eligible protected decodes have been considered.

The remaining common budget is filled in stable order with additional decodes and page-aligned
prefill/recompute chunks according to the selected mode and forced-phase counters. One request appears
at most once in a step.

### 13.2 Alternating mode

Alternating mode produces a phase-homogeneous M7-compatible step:

- choose decode when protected decode work exists, except when the forced-prefill bound applies;
- choose prompt/recompute when forced or when no decode item fits;
- otherwise choose the phase of the highest-ranked eligible head;
- fill only that phase using the shared exact budgets; and
- increment/reset consecutive-phase counters only after accepted submission.

Prompt and recompute may share a prefill-shaped batch only when their metadata and logits-selection
requirements form one prepared M4 key. Per-item `emit_logits` remains explicit.

### 13.3 Mixed mode

Mixed mode may place decode and prefill/recompute items in one step only when startup preparation has
selected an implementation that declares:

- ragged positions and causal metadata for both classes;
- correct prior-KV reads and append ranges for every item;
- explicit selected-logit rows, with no row for intermediate/recompute items;
- finite workspace/metadata bounds for the combined shape; and
- correctness coverage for all admitted page sizes and boundary shapes.

Protected decodes are inserted first, then prefill/recompute fills the residual token budget. Plan
item order is canonical: work class order followed by section 11 order, independent of hash storage.
The device completion may preserve that canonical order or use explicit item ordinals; coordinator
commit still follows M7's defined completion order.

If mixed capability is absent, configuration resolves to alternating before admission. A launch-time
failure never retries the same plan in another mode.

## 14. Low-water handling and recompute preemption

### 14.1 Trigger and goals

The coordinator considers preemption when M6 reports low water and either:

- a higher-ranked eligible head cannot prepare its required quota/pages;
- the protected decode set cannot prepare an append; or
- the free-block forecast is below the configured exit threshold for the next worst-case step.

The reclaim goal is the checked maximum of the blocked proposal's shortage,
`preemption_min_reclaim_blocks`, and the blocks required to exit low water with hysteresis. If no work
is blocked and the forecast is safe, low water alone records pressure but does not churn requests.

### 14.2 Eligible victims

A victim must:

- be in prompt-ready, recompute-ready, or decode-ready state;
- have no in-flight ticket, pending append, completion awaiting acknowledgement, pin, cancellation
  drain, or terminal emission;
- own a committed M6 table and quota that can be shortened transactionally;
- retain immutable token history sufficient to rebuild released KV; and
- not be one of the protected requests in the proposal that triggered reclaim.

M8 never interrupts a GPU kernel and never releases uncertain or pinned pages. If pressure begins
while every useful victim is in flight, the scheduler waits for an ordered completion and retries.

### 14.3 Deterministic victim order

Eligible victims sort by:

1. effective priority ascending;
2. deadline protection: no deadline, non-urgent deadline, then urgent deadline;
3. work protection: prefill/recompute before decode;
4. reclaimable blocks descending;
5. recomputation cost ascending;
6. arrival time descending; and
7. strong request ID descending.

`reclaimable blocks` and `recomputation cost` are exact integers calculated from the same resource
snapshot. Selection takes the shortest prefix of this order that reaches the reclaim goal, bounded by
`preemption_max_victims_per_turn`. There is no randomized victim choice.

### 14.4 Safe retained prefix

For each victim, the policy first calculates the largest candidate keep length that:

- does not exceed `preemption_keep_blocks * page_size`;
- consists only of full, committed, ready, unpinned pages from logical position zero;
- has generations/table entries matching the current request epoch and table epoch;
- leaves enough released blocks to contribute to the reclaim goal; and
- fits the reduced quota retained by the request.

The keep length is therefore zero or page-aligned. The exclusive partial tail is always released.
No hole, suffix, tentative append, poisoned page, or page owned by another request can be retained.
The default of zero implements full recomputation and is the correctness baseline.

### 14.5 Atomic preemption transition

A prefix-reclaim transaction prepares, without visible mutation:

- victim lifecycle transitions into preempted/requeue states;
- a checked new request epoch;
- cancellation of the old ready eligibility interval;
- the retained table prefix and new table epoch;
- release sets for table owners, quota, and physical pages;
- new `computed_tokens == cached_tokens == keep_length`;
- `recompute_target_tokens == old_computed_tokens`;
- the original post-recompute destination work kind; and
- all trace/metric records.

After all selected victim transactions and the newly unblocked proposal are prepared and validated,
backend acceptance is the commit boundary. Commit transfers the retained table to the new request
epoch, releases the suffix, applies counters, and requeues victims deterministically. If no execution
proposal is accepted in the same turn, a standalone reclaim may commit only under an ADR-defined
coordinator transaction whose capacity benefit and rollback are independently tested; the initial
implementation should couple reclaim to a blocked valid proposal.

On re-admission, the request enters recompute-ready when `computed_tokens < recompute_target_tokens`.
It rebuilds exact chunks without emitting tokens, then returns to its recorded prompt-ready or
decode-ready destination. Already committed output and response sequence numbers never roll back.

### 14.6 No victim and churn control

If the target cannot be met within eligible victims and the per-turn bound:

- roll back all prepared victim changes;
- keep the blocked request ready/queued without partial resource charge;
- emit a bounded `no_allocatable_victim` decision with reason counts;
- arm progress on completion, release, cancellation, or policy update rather than busy-spin; and
- fail only if capacity analysis proves the request can never fit the configured engine, not merely
  because resources are temporarily busy.

Hysteresis, minimum reclaim, per-turn victim limit, and per-request preemption count are observable.
The initial policy may cap repeated preemptions of one request; once capped, it is protected as a
victim but remains schedulable. Any cap must be part of the snapshot and fairness proof.

## 15. Atomic plan, reservation, and commit protocol

M8 fully wires the two-phase transaction promised by M1/M6/M7. No step is partially scheduled.

### 15.1 Read phase

Capture one coherent coordinator-owned view containing:

- policy generation and monotonic time;
- eligible request IDs, epochs, states, counters, ready times, priority, and deadlines;
- step/sequence/token/workspace/metadata/response/plan-buffer capacities;
- M6 quota, block-table, physical-free, low-water, generation, and pin state;
- selected backend capability and device health; and
- consecutive-phase and estimator state.

Because the coordinator is the sole writer, this is a logical snapshot rather than a lock-based copy.
Every input affecting the decision is traceable.

### 15.2 Pure proposal phase

Calculate ranking, deadline classes, protected decode set, chunks, phase mode, possible victims,
resource deltas, and canonical plan items without mutating request or allocator state. The proposal
contains both execution and reclaim actions plus an explanation code for every eligible item not
selected.

### 15.3 Prepare phase

Prepare in a documented fixed order:

1. plan-buffer lease and completion-channel capacity;
2. per-request lifecycle transitions and scheduled ranges;
3. response slots needed by possible output items;
4. victim prefix-reclaim transactions, if any;
5. M6 quota/table append transactions and device-mirror deltas;
6. workspace, activation, metadata, event, and device-ticket leases;
7. immutable physical plan and full validator pass; and
8. worker-channel acceptance reservation.

Each prepared object is move-only, uniquely identified, and has a no-throw/no-fail rollback that
returns exactly its tentative ownership. Public snapshots continue to show only committed state.
When a blocked append depends on victim reclamation, one composite transaction may spend only the
suffix/quota credit staged by those exact victim transactions; validator totals must net the staged
release and new charge without exposing either one separately.

### 15.4 Acceptance and no-fail commit

The device submission protocol must distinguish reservation from accepted ownership. Once the
immutable submission is durably accepted by the worker channel:

1. commit reclaim transactions and epoch/table transfers;
2. commit append/ticket/workspace/metadata ownership;
3. apply lifecycle submit transitions and set exact `scheduled_tokens`;
4. install the in-flight ticket and policy-snapshot reference;
5. update phase counters and trace ordinal; and
6. publish the accepted-plan event.

All fallible work precedes this point. Commit consists only of already-validated bounded mutations.
After acceptance, every worker path returns a matching completion/drain record; it never silently
rejects ownership.

### 15.5 Rollback

Before acceptance, any failure rolls back in exact reverse preparation order. Rollback must restore:

- request state/epoch/counters/ready time and queue indexes;
- quota, block owners, physical-free list, table generations/epochs, pins, and mirror staging;
- workspace, metadata, event, plan-buffer, ticket, response, and channel capacity;
- phase counters, estimator state, and policy reference counts; and
- the same canonical invariant snapshot observed before prepare.

Rollback does not emit a token, terminal response, policy commit, or preemption metric. It emits one
bounded proposal-rejection trace after state is restored. Property tests compare the full normalized
snapshot before and after every injected prepare failure.

## 16. Completion, cancellation, and stale work

A completion is eligible to mutate state only when request ID, sequence ID, request epoch, step ID,
ticket ID, work kind, logical range, scheduled count, table identity/epoch, policy generation, item
ordinal, and final fence all match.

On successful intermediate prompt/recompute completion, commit only KV and computed progress. On
successful final prompt or decode completion, follow M5/M7 output, EOS, finish, response ordering,
and acknowledgement rules. On safe execution failure, abort the append and clear scheduled work
through the matching lifecycle transition. On an uncertain device failure, poison/drain the full
accepted batch as M2/M7 require.

Cancellation or deadline expiry racing with chunk completion follows M7 event precedence:

- if cancellation/expiry is observed before completion in the same coordinator turn, validate and
  drain the completion without committing output or new progress, then release safely;
- if completion commits first, its ordered response/progress is real and later cancellation acts on
  the new state; and
- exactly one terminal event is emitted in either case.

A late completion from a pre-preemption epoch cannot match because preemption is legal only with no
in-flight work. Fault injection nevertheless supplies stale/duplicate records; they are quarantined,
acknowledged as appropriate, and cannot mutate the new table or counters.

## 18. Trace, replay, and observability

M8 evolves the trace schema with backward readers or an explicit conversion tool. Each decision
records:

- turn/event ordinal, clock value, policy generation/digest, estimator sample ordinal;
- coherent resource/low-water snapshot and phase counters;
- each eligible request's identity, state, counters, priority/age/effective priority, deadline class,
  and canonical rank;
- decode protection, selected work kind/range, chunk math inputs, budgets before/after, and rejection
  reason;
- victim candidates, exact sort keys, retained/released ranges, reclaim goal, and chosen prefix;
- every prepared transaction identity and prepare/accept/commit/rollback result;
- immutable logical plan digest and backend mode/key; and
- completion validation result and post-commit normalized invariant digest.

Production telemetry remains bounded and does not serialize token values or unbounded queue dumps.
Detailed traces are opt-in, capacity-limited, and follow M7 privacy/backpressure rules.

Required metrics include:

- chunk count/size/final-tail distribution by work kind;
- ready queue depth and wait histograms by priority/deadline/work class;
- phase/mixed batch counts, decode reserve requested/used, and forced-phase events;
- deadline admissions/rejections/expiry/late completions and estimator error;
- low-water entry/duration/exit, blocked proposals, victim failures, and reclaim latency;
- preemptions per request, blocks retained/released, and recompute-token ratio;
- prepare failures and rollback duration by resource stage;
- planner wall/CPU time, eligible scans, candidates considered, and plan fill ratio; and
- all M7/M6 resource current/peak/baseline counters.

Request IDs are sampled/hashed under existing telemetry policy. Priority/deadline labels must be
bounded to avoid cardinality explosions.

## 19. Failure behavior

| Failure | Required result |
|---|---|
| invalid chunk/policy configuration | startup or update rejected; prior snapshot remains active |
| non-final chunk cannot reach a page boundary | item does not fit this step; no zero-length schedule |
| impossible deadline at admission | stable rejection, no admitted-resource leak |
| deadline becomes at-risk after admission | continue best-effort until actual expiry; record estimate |
| plan validation or worker reservation rejection | reverse rollback to byte-equivalent normalized state |
| worker failure before accepted ownership | rollback proposal; request remains ready unless error is fatal |
| execution failure mid-chunk after acceptance | matching completion aborts append/scheduled progress; no partial commit |
| low water with pinned/in-flight candidates | wait for ordered progress; never free or busy-spin |
| no allocatable victim | rollback all victims; defer blocked work with reason |
| reclaim transaction prepare failure | no victim mutation or partial page release |
| stale preempted completion/table epoch | reject/quarantine; no new-epoch mutation |
| cancellation during chunk | drain by precedence; one terminal; all pages/tickets released safely |
| policy update during in-flight step | new turns use new generation; ticket completes under retained old snapshot |
| estimator invalid sample or arithmetic overflow | discard sample or reject estimate conservatively; never NaN-order work |
| mixed backend unavailable | startup/config rejection or resolved alternating mode; no runtime fallback |
| trace/metrics sink failure | scheduling truth commits; sink disables under M7 bounded failure policy |

Every failure has a stable status category and bounded diagnostic context. Temporary resource pressure
is not reported as internal failure, and a permanent never-fits request is not left queued forever.

## 20. Test plan

### 20.1 Unit tests

- Chunk range math for every page/chunk/context/budget boundary and checked overflow.
- Final-tail handling and proof that intermediate ranges are page-aligned and nonempty.
- Counter transitions for prompt, recompute, decode, cancellation, failure, and terminal cleanup.
- Priority validation, aging quanta/cap, clock edge, stable total-order tie-breakers, and ready reset.
- Deadline estimate, guard, overflow, urgent class, impossible rejection, and estimator snapshot update.
- Decode reserve and both consecutive-phase bounds under exact sequence/token/workspace limits.
- Mixed capability resolution and canonical item order.
- Victim eligibility, exact sort order, reclaim target, retained prefix, partial-tail release, and caps.
- Two-phase prepare failure at every stage with full normalized before/after equality.
- Policy parse/validation/canonical digest/generation/update lifetime and replay compatibility.
- Import schema parsing, malformed/overflow inputs, event precedence, and deterministic output.

### 20.2 Integration tests

- A page-boundary-heavy long prompt interleaves with one and many decode streams.
- Alternating and supported mixed mode add/remove requests across iterations under backpressure.
- Low water preempts prompt-ready and decode-ready victims, retains zero/multiple safe pages, rebuilds
  history, and returns to the correct state.
- Cancellation/deadline/drop races at proposal, prepare, accepted, mid-chunk completion, recompute,
  and terminal emission.
- Policy update while requests are queued, ready, preempted, and in flight.
- M6 quota/page pressure clears after completion/cancellation and wakes blocked scheduling once.
- Graceful drain/cancel shutdown with chunked, recomputing, and old-policy tickets.

### 20.3 Correctness tests

For every supported page size and selected backend:

- compare unchunked M7, each legal chunk size, and random legal decompositions at every committed KV
  boundary, final hidden state, logits, greedy token, EOS result, and terminal output;
- compare uninterrupted execution with full-recompute and retained-prefix preemption at every legal
  victim boundary;
- cover prompts of 1, around every page/chunk boundary, near model context, and with multiple output
  lengths including immediate EOS;
- cover batches with uneven prompt/history lengths and changing mixed/alternating composition; and
- require token-identical results under the declared numeric policy, not merely similar text.

Where floating reduction order differs for a capability-approved mixed backend, the ADR must either
prove exact greedy-token stability over the complete fixture corpus and retain bounded numeric
evidence, or declare that mode unsupported. The milestone completion criterion remains token-identical.

### 20.4 Stress and property tests

- Thirty-minute adversarial long/short, minimum/maximum priority, deadline/no-deadline mix with a
  continuously eligible tagged request and automated starvation assertion.
- Near-full KV with repeated page-boundary decode, low-water oscillation, victim caps, recomputation,
  cancellation storms, and slow consumers.
- Randomized event schedules with manual clock, policy updates, prepare failures, duplicate/stale
  completion, and invariant check after every event.
- Thousands of requests and a two-hour nightly soak with final zero/baseline resource equality.
- Property generation/shrinking for chunk decomposition, victim sets, resource transactions, and
  replay determinism; minimized seeds retained as fixtures.

### 20.5 Failure injection

Inject failure before and after every fallible prepare stage, worker-channel reservation, accepted
submission, device launch, final fence, append completion, response reservation, reclaim commit, and
acknowledgement. Include:

- plan rejection after one or many victim/append reservations;
- execution failure on first/intermediate/final chunk;
- no eligible or no sufficient victim;
- impossible and just-feasible deadline;
- stale request/table/policy generation completion;
- cancellation before and after the same completion ordinal; and
- device poison during a mixed batch.

Every case asserts one terminal outcome where applicable, no partial progress, no duplicate response,
safe fence-aware reuse, and exact counter/pool baselines.

### 20.6 Sanitizers and static checks

- ASan/UBSan for policy updates and failure/property suites.
- TSan for engine commands, snapshot lifetime, cancellation/deadline, and shutdown stress.
- Compute Sanitizer memcheck/racecheck for all supported chunk/page/mixed boundary paths.
- clang-tidy/IWYU/header self-containment/no-CUDA-in-generic-layer checks.
- Fuzzing for policy/workload/trace parsers and normalized replay comparison.

Required sanitizer/GPU tests fail rather than silently skip on their owned CI lane.

## 21. Performance methodology and gates

The declared primary workload must be checked into benchmark metadata and contain, at minimum:

- a reproducible mixture of long prompts and active decode streams;
- arrival process, concurrency, prompt/output distributions, priority/deadline distribution, and
  response-consumer behavior;
- model, precision, page size, memory budget, hardware/software clocks and versions;
- warmup, trial duration/count, random seeds, SLO definition, and confidence method; and
- M7 unchunked FCFS as the paired baseline.

Sweeps cover chunk sizes from one page through the unchunked limit, scheduled-token budget, decode
reserve, forced-phase intervals, alternating/mixed capability, low-water thresholds, and retention.
Measure goodput, SLO goodput, TTFT, TPOT/inter-token p50/p95/p99/max, end-to-end latency, GPU
utilization, batch fill, scheduler p50/p99 cost, recompute amplification, preemption cost, and deadline
outcomes.

The selected default must:

- improve p99 TPOT by at least 20% versus unchunked prefill on the declared mixed workload;
- reduce SLO goodput by no more than 5%;
- keep all correctness and fairness gates passing; and
- retain coordinator p99 planning evidence against M7's 1 ms objective at 1,024 active requests.

If no configuration meets the first two gates, chunking remains implemented capability-only, the
measured best safe/default policy is documented, and the ADR records the limiting bottleneck. Results
must not be cherry-picked by changing workload, SLO, or baseline after measurement.

## 22. Implementation sequence

### M8.1 — Counter and chunk semantics

Extend typed work kinds, lifecycle transitions, exact counters, validators, trace schema, and pure
page-aligned chunk calculation. Gate: CPU unit/property/replay tests and unchanged M7 behavior when
chunking is disabled.

### M8.2 — Fair scheduling and deadline policy

Add immutable policy snapshots, priority aging, stable order, deadline estimator/admission, decode
reservation, and forced-phase counters. Gate: manual-clock deterministic fairness/deadline fixtures
with no eligible starvation.

### M8.3 — Chunked device execution and phase capability

Wire prompt/recompute ranges, selected logits, alternating mode, and capability-gated mixed mode into
M5/M6/M7 plans. Gate: all page/chunk/batch decompositions token-identical to M7 plus required GPU
sanitizer coverage.

### M8.4 — Low-water recompute preemption

Add ready-victim policy, prefix-reclaim transaction, epoch/table transfer, history recomputation,
churn controls, and exact rollback. Gate: property/failure matrix proves safe prefixes, stale
rejection, no leaks, and preempted-vs-uninterrupted equality.

### M8.5 — Stress and default selection

Add adversarial/soak suites, benchmark sweeps, evidence manifests, and
operator documentation. Gate: definition of done and accepted ADRs with data-driven default/handoff.

Each stage merges with its tests and documentation. A later stage must not weaken an earlier
invariant to meet performance targets.

## 24. Risks and experiments

| Risk | Required evidence | Decision/fallback |
|---|---|---|
| page-aligned chunks waste small residual budgets | budget/page/chunk sweep and fill traces | tune step/chunk budget; never create misaligned interior chunks |
| chunking lowers TTFT or goodput through launch overhead | paired workload sweep and profiler timeline | retain capability with evidence-driven larger/default chunks |
| decode reserve starves prompt work | 30-minute adversarial soak with forced-phase counters | tune reserve/interval while preserving explicit bound |
| priority aging defeats priority intent | wait distributions and formal rank examples | cap aging explicitly; document effective-priority semantics |
| deadline estimator rejects viable work | confusion matrix and rate perturbation tests | conservative versioned calibration; expose best-effort status |
| preemption causes recompute thrash | amplification/victim/hysteresis sweep | protect repeatedly preempted work, raise minimum reclaim, or default retention zero |
| retained pages fail to reclaim enough | exact goal fixtures under fragmented tails | reduce keep length deterministically; rollback if goal still unmet |
| mixed kernels change numeric order | intermediate/logit/token corpus across shapes | default alternating or declare mixed shape unsupported |
| two-phase prepare adds coordinator latency | stage-level p99 profile at 1,024 active | bound scans/candidates and reuse fixed storage; keep atomicity |
| old policy snapshot leaks through tickets | update/in-flight/shutdown lifetime tests | refcount by accepted ownership and release on acknowledgement |
| no victim leads to busy loop | wake-source and coordinator CPU stress | sleep until resource/event change; never spin |
| full history storage is insufficient for decode recompute | counter/history convention proof | preserve committed token IDs for request lifetime; reject unsafe preemption |

## 26. Definition of done

### Policy and progress

- [ ] Every prompt/recompute chunk obeys exact range, page-boundary, final-tail, context, and budget
      rules; no zero-length item is possible.
- [ ] Retained, computed, scheduled, target-history, recompute-target, and committed-output counters
      match M6 table length and request vectors at every observable state.
- [ ] Priority/aging/deadline ranking is a deterministic total order from one immutable snapshot.
- [ ] Decode reservation and forced-phase limits protect both decode and prefill progress.
- [ ] Mixed mode is selected only by prepared capability; alternating mode remains complete.

### Preemption and transactions

- [ ] Only ready, unpinned, fully validated victims are selected in the documented order.
- [ ] Retention contains only a same-request, full-page, committed prefix; partial/tentative/stale pages
      are released or rejected.
- [ ] Recomputation emits no output and returns token-identically to the saved destination state.
- [ ] Every prepare rejection restores the full normalized request/KV/resource snapshot exactly.
- [ ] Every accepted plan has one matching completion/drain path and stale identities cannot mutate
      committed state.

### Correctness, lifecycle, and fairness

- [ ] Chunked versus unchunked and preempted versus uninterrupted fixtures are token-identical for all
      supported page sizes and boundary-heavy inputs.
- [ ] Cancellation/deadline/failure/shutdown at every chunk/recompute state emits one terminal outcome
      and releases all ownership after safe drain.
- [ ] No continuously eligible request starves in the 30-minute adversarial run; the trace proves the
      configured priority/deadline policy was followed.
- [ ] Completed + cancelled + failed equals admitted, and all request/KV/workspace/ticket/response/
      snapshot counters return to baseline after tests and soak.

### Evidence and performance

- [ ] Unit, integration, correctness, stress, failure, property, sanitizer, and replay suites pass
      without hidden skips on required lanes.
- [ ] Importer and comparison tool reproduce decisions and paired metrics from canonical workloads.
- [ ] Selected defaults meet the >=20% p99 TPOT improvement and <=5% SLO-goodput loss gates, or the
      capability-only result and honest limiting evidence are recorded exactly as the roadmap allows.
- [ ] Coordinator planning, low-water, preemption, recompute, deadline, and policy-update telemetry is
      bounded and documented.
- [ ] ADRs 0049-0055, operator documentation, schemas, fixtures, and benchmark evidence match the
      implementation.

## 27. Handoff to M9

M8 hands M9 a deterministic, bounded request engine whose long prompts cannot monopolize execution,
whose ready work has documented priority/deadline/fairness behavior, whose paged KV can be reclaimed
and recomputed safely under pressure, and whose policy decisions are snapshot-versioned and replayable.

M9 may add complete baseline sampling and HTTP/SSE protocol behavior. It must not bypass M8 admission
or queue ordering, treat an accepted deadline as a hard guarantee, mutate scheduler policy outside an
ordered coordinator command, duplicate output during recomputation, or map internal preemption into a
client-visible token rollback. HTTP disconnect, timeout, output limits, and backpressure must continue
through M7/M8's cancellation, terminal-event, resource-drain, and exact-accounting paths.
